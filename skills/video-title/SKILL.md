# Video titles

A title does the same job as the opening sentence, for a different sense: it is **read, not heard**, usually before or without the video playing. It shows up in search results, suggested videos, the channel page, shares in chats, and small under a short in the feed. Whoever reads it decides in a glance whether to watch.

Two failures kept repeating when agents wrote titles without a skill:

- **The rules were right and then broken.** Agents set themselves "no clickbait, true, don't repeat the hook", then shipped the spoken hook word for word as the title, or put a claim in the title that they had rejected as a myth for the hook a few lines earlier.
- **Filler instead of information:** "Worship band still on WhatsApp? Watch this", "Tohle zkus", "The setlist app for church worship bands" (a catalogue label, not a reason to watch).

## 1. The shared rules (same as the opening sentence)

These are the rules of `shorts-hook` section 1, applied to the title. If one changes there, change it here too.

1. **The first words do work.** Start on the strongest concrete thing: a number, the object, the outcome, the question. Don't open with "This", "So", "Today", "In this video", "Have you ever wondered", "Věděli jste, že", "Dnes si povíme", a date or the bare topic name.
2. **Concrete beats general.** "Why your battery % suddenly jumps" beats "The truth about batteries".
3. **Stakes, a result, a contradiction or a real question.** Something is lost, won, broken, saved, or a question the reader actually has.
4. **A promise the video keeps.** The video answers the title. A title the video doesn't pay off is clickbait: people leave and remember.
5. **True.** Check the title like any other claim, on its own. A fact you rejected for the hook doesn't come back in the title.
6. **Understandable cold.** Someone who has never heard of the topic understands every word and why it would matter to them. No unexplained jargon, abbreviation, insider reference, or contradiction of a myth only some people know ("The Chernobyl divers didn't die"). No riddle.
7. **Vary the type across a channel.** Not every title "X is lying to you".

## 2. What is specific to a title

- **Length.** YouTube allows long titles but cuts them in most views, and how much shows depends on the device and the surface. Put the point in the first ~40 characters and keep the whole title under ~60. Check that the cut version still makes sense.
- **A search term near the front.** The word people would type: the name, the object, the question ("49,90", "Jan Palach", "battery percentage"). Here a title differs from the spoken hook: a known name helps search, so it may lead, but the rest of the title says in plain words what happened or what the video answers. "Jan Palach: what he actually demanded in 1969" works for a reader who has never heard the name; "Jan Palach" alone doesn't.
- **Complement, don't copy.** The title, the on-screen hook text and the spoken hook make the same promise in different words. A common split: the title carries the searchable question, the hook carries the stakes. A title identical to the spoken hook wastes one of the two.
- **Question titles** fit explainers well when they are the question people really ask ("Proč všechno stojí 49,90, a ne 50 korun?"). The video must answer it early.
- **No filler and no shouting:** "Watch this", "You won't believe", "Tohle zkus", "Šokující", "(GONE WRONG)", whole words in capitals, a row of emoji, a trailing "!!!".
- **Capitalisation and language.** Czech titles capitalise only the first word and proper names ("Proč ceny končí na ,90", not "Proč Ceny Končí Na ,90"). English may use title case or sentence case; keep the channel's choice. Always correct diacritics. Use the same tykání or vykání as the video.
- **Products.** Don't lead with a product name nobody knows; lead with the pain or the outcome the viewer recognises. The name can come later in the title or in the description.
- **Sensitive topics** follow `shorts-hook` ("Sensitive topics"): aim at the person, the motive or what changed, never the method or the injury, no tabloid words.
- **Shorts vs long videos.** In the Shorts feed the video starts playing and the hook does most of the work; the title matters for search, for the channel page and for shares. For a long video the title (together with the thumbnail) *is* the hook: spend more effort on it, and make the thumbnail and the title say different things that add up to one promise.

## 3. How to write it

1. Write the title **after the hook and the payoff are fixed**, so you know which promise the video keeps.
2. Write **8-10 candidates across at least four types**:

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| The question people search | "Why does your battery % suddenly jump?" / "Proč všechno stojí 49,90, a ne 50 korun?" |
| Outcome first | "Three men went under Chernobyl's reactor and walked out" |
| Contradiction (of a belief most people hold) | "0% doesn't mean your battery is empty" |
| Name + what happened in plain words | "Co přesně chtěl Jan Palach v lednu 1969" |
| Number / list | "3 things that drain your battery in the cold" |
| Direct address / pain | "Kapela se domlouvá přes WhatsApp? Neděle nemusí být chaos" |

3. Score each candidate with **evidence, not ticks**:
   - **First ~40 characters:** quote them. Do they hold the point on their own?
   - **Search term:** which word would someone type, and where is it?
   - **Cold:** which word, if any, would a stranger to the topic not understand?
   - **True:** which fact does it rest on, and is it checked? Did this claim fail the check anywhere else in your work?
   - **Paid off:** at which second does the video answer it?
   - **Differs from the hook:** quote the spoken hook and the on-screen text next to it.
   - **No filler:** no word you could delete without losing information.
4. Keep the best one and two alternatives, and tell the user all three with one line on why.

## 4. Weak vs strong

| Weak | Why | Stronger |
|---|---|---|
| "The Chernobyl Divers Didn't Die" | the spoken hook word for word; needs the viewer to know a TV myth | "What really happened to the three men under Chernobyl" |
| "Why Your Phone Dies at 20% in the Cold" | "dies at 20%" is folklore, not a spec; the author had flagged it as such | "Why your phone dies early in the cold" |
| "Worship band still on WhatsApp? Watch this" | "Watch this" carries nothing | "Every musician in their own key, one setlist" |
| "The setlist app for church worship bands" | a catalogue label, no reason to watch | "Změň tóninu jednou a přepne se všem v kapele" |
| "Jan Palach nechtěl obdiv. Chtěl konec cenzury." | an interpretation presented as fact | "Co přesně chtěl Jan Palach v lednu 1969" |
| "Ceny na ,90: trik, který na tebe funguje" | vague "trik"; says nothing concrete | "Proč všechno stojí 49,90, a ne 50 korun?" |

## 5. After publishing

Tell the user where to look, and that YouTube publishes no benchmarks, so compare with the channel's own videos:

- **Long videos:** impressions click-through rate in YouTube Studio, read together with average view duration. A high click rate with a short view duration means the title promised something the video didn't deliver. If Studio offers testing titles against each other (Test & compare) for the channel, use it.
- **Shorts:** the traffic source "YouTube search" and views from the channel page. The feed is driven mostly by the hook ("Viewed vs. swiped away"), so changing a short's title rarely changes the feed.

## Before sending

- The title was picked from 8-10 written candidates across at least four types, and the user got the pick plus two alternatives.
- The point sits in the first ~40 characters; the whole is under ~60.
- A stranger to the topic understands it; the search term is near the front.
- Every claim in it is checked, including ones you dropped from the hook.
- It doesn't repeat the spoken hook or the on-screen text word for word, and has no filler, capitals or emoji.
- Capitalisation and diacritics are correct for the language, and the address matches the video.

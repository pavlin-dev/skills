# Engaging short videos

A viewer gives a short video about one second to say what it is, then about one second per idea. Every frame either explains the product or carries the viewer to the next frame. A slideshow of nice stills is boring, however good the stills are.

This skill is about what to make and how to judge it. How to build it (Remotion, footage, music, rendering on this machine) is in the `video-production` skill.

## Think like the viewer, critically

Your main job is not to produce frames but to judge them the way the person scrolling a feed will. Do it at every checkpoint (plan, stills, final contact sheet) and write down what fails before fixing it:

- **First second:** with no context and the sound off, do I know what this is and why I should care?
- **Every second after:** is something new happening? Anything static or unclear for more than ~1.5 s is where people swipe away.
- **Each caption:** would someone who has never seen the product get it instantly? Is it their words, not the maker's?
- **Each cut:** does it feel caused by something moving, or does the video just jump?
- **The owner's eyes:** is every claim true and taken from the product's own site? Does anything misrepresent the product or a competitor?
- **The end:** do I know what to do next and where?

Don't defend your own draft. Say plainly what is weak, also in the final message to the user, and list what you could not check (you cannot listen to the music).

## Techniques that work

1. **Say what it is in frame 0.** Category and promise readable at t=0 ("Claude Design → MP4"), the key word (VR, AI, MP4…) emphasized. Start entrance animations at negative time.
2. **One subject**, one demo, 15–25 s (default 20 s). Not a feature tour.
3. **One connecting motif**: an object from the product (a ball, the logo's play-dot, a cursor) travels through the whole video and causes every transition: it bounces on words, smashes into the lens, wipes a frame, acts as the pointer, becomes the logo.
4. **No plain cuts.** Iris, zoom-through, whip-pan push, match cut. Keep elements alive across cuts.
5. **Everything on the beat.** Music with a clear kick; landings, cuts, reveals and flashes on beats; scene lengths in bars.
6. **Show the real product**: real footage or real UI states, never invented screens or numbers.
7. **Short text for the viewer**: max ~4 words per caption, one per scene, big, brand-coloured full stop. Follow `user-facing-copy`.
8. **One moment of contrast**: slow-mo, a breakdown, or problem → fix (the "before" muffled and ugly, the motif wipes it away, music and flash hit on the beat).
9. **A sound for every motion**, under the music.
10. **A clear CTA** held 3–4 s: logo, one promise, a button, the URL.

## Process

1. Study the product on its own site: what it does, brand colours, logo, claims, the real flow.
2. Write the plan (storyboard in beats, motif, captions) for the user; don't wait for approval on small choices.
3. Build, look at stills of frame 0, every landing and every cut, critique as the viewer, fix, repeat.
4. Render, critique the contact sheet once more, send the video with an honest list of weak spots and unchecked things.
